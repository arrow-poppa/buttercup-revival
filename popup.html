Subtitle layout

Following the speaker ends each subtitle with the sentence, at a comma once it is fairly full, or at a breath, so subtitles appear and leave with the speech. The word limit below still caps their size.

Max words per subtitle: how many words stay on screen together before the next subtitle starts (a pause longer than 1 s also starts a new one). Needs word timestamps (keep “Request word timestamps” on); a model that returns none keeps its own, often long, segments. Line break: only wraps that subtitle into lines. Neither changes what is sent to the API, so you can adjust them without paying for a new transcription.

Audio upload (advanced)

How the audio is sent to the API. This does not change how subtitles look.

Sends the complete audio once, without cuts by Buttercup. It must fit the limit below and be accepted by the model. If the request fails there is no automatic resend; you may need to raise the timeout. The provider may still split the audio internally.

Sends parts to the same provider in parallel and keeps the subtitle order. On an error it starts no more parts and tries no other provider. Requests already sent may be charged by the API.

A limit per request, not per video: about 45 minutes of audio fit in 24 MB. Parts overlap by 1.5 s so no word is lost at a cut. Groq and OpenRouter reject uploaded files above 25 MB, also on paid plans; raise this only for an API that accepts more.
